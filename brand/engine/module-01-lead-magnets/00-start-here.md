# Start Here: why this works, and whether you should bother

**Draft in Mauro's voice, 21 Aug 2026. Correct anything that isn't how you'd say it.**

Read this before the rules. If you skip to the rules you'll build a magnet that looks right and doesn't convert, because the reasoning is what tells you which decisions matter.

---

## The one thing a lead magnet does

It converts attention into a conversation you can see.

That's it. Not authority, not reach, not warmth. A named artifact that someone has to raise a hand to claim, and the raised hand is a person with an intent you can act on the same day.

Impressions don't tell you anything. A post can do 59,000 views and produce fewer conversations than one that did 21,000. I have the numbers on that and they're in the next page. What tells you something is how many people asked for the thing.

**So the number to watch is the comment rate, not the views.** Comments are the claim action. That's the one that maps to a conversation, and a conversation is the only thing that maps to money.

---

## Why most of them fail

Two separate effects get treated as one.

**Breadth drives reach.** The broadest word in your niche pulls the highest view count. "AI" beat everything on my account.

**A concrete, currently-urgent named thing drives redemption.** Those posts got roughly four times the comment rate of the broad ones, on a third of the views.

People optimise for the first and then wonder why nobody claimed anything. You want a title broad enough that a beginner recognises the topic and concrete enough that it feels urgent this month. Both, in the same line. Most titles pick one.

The second failure is quieter. The magnet gets built as something to read. Rules, frameworks, an explanation. The reader nods, learns something, closes it, and their Monday looks identical. **If someone can read the whole thing and do nothing differently tomorrow, it failed**, and the writing quality is irrelevant to that.

## Why it keeps working when other things stop

Because it's built out of work you already did.

The input isn't an idea. It's a transcript, a screen recording, a doc you made for a client, a thing you figured out last Tuesday. That already exists, so the marginal cost of the next magnet is near zero. That's the whole reason this survives contact with a real week. Anything that needs net-new thinking gets skipped the first time you're busy, and you're busy most weeks.

Roughly fifteen minutes of my own time per magnet once the source material is in hand. Not fifteen minutes to learn the system. Fifteen minutes to run it.

## The part nobody tells you

They fatigue, and faster than you would guess.

Five auto-DMs, one account, one week: **7,839 → 6,329 → 4,369 → 1,637 → 901**. The fifth did eleven percent of the first, six days apart.

The reason is not the mechanism. The first one beat every long-form post published that week except one. The reason is that four of the five were the same asset with the same opening line.

**Rotate the shape, don't cut the volume.** One of any given asset type per week, maximum. The instinct when performance drops is to post less. That's backwards.

---

## Whether you should test this at all

Say no if any of these is true.

**You don't have a body of real work to package.** This system turns existing work into assets. With nothing behind you, you'd be inventing content and dressing it as proof, and a sharp reader catches that immediately. Go do the work first.

**You can't name a specific problem your buyer has this month.** Not a category, a problem. If the best you have is "marketing," the title has nothing concrete to grab and the redemption rate dies.

**You have nowhere for the conversation to go.** A raised hand with no next step is worse than no raised hand, because you've spent the goodwill and got nothing. Have something to say when they reply, even if it's just a question.

**You want reach more than conversations.** Fair, but then this is the wrong tool. Post takes, not magnets.

Say yes if you already produce real work, you can name the live problem, and you can hold a conversation when someone raises their hand. Everything after this page is mechanics.

## How to use the rest of this

1. **Install** — get Claude set up. Three paths, pick one, none of them need a terminal.
2. **Your Brand Context** — the one input the system needs from you. Fill it in once, every magnet after that inherits it.
3. **The Rules** — the title formula, the $100 bar, structure, images, cover, fatigue, measurement. The what.
4. **The Skill Files** — the actual files, in full. Copy them in and the system runs.
5. **The Templates** — the post, the DM, the auto-DM config, the cover spec, the in-asset prompt blocks.
6. **The Worked Example** — one magnet built start to finish, so you can see what done looks like.
7. **Score Your Own** — 20 checks and a verdict. Run it before you ship, not after.
8. **The Google Doc Format** — the second delivery lane, for when you want a document rather than a workspace.

**Your first build will take a few hours.** Every one after that is the fifteen minutes. Don't judge the system on the first run.
