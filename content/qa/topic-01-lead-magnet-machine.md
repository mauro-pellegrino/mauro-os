# Topic 1: A lead magnet isn't the asset, it's the machine around it

**For:** Juan
**Answered by:** Mauro, 24 Aug 2026
**Status:** raw Q&A, Mauro's voice, unedited source material. Not a post.

**The flag:** everyone ships a PDF and waits. The machine turns a comment into a booked call.

---

**1. Walk me through the whole machine, from the second someone comments the keyword to the moment they book a call.**

When they comment on a lead magnet, they are literally showing interest in your resource and what you have to offer. Getting a lot of comments is the beginning of getting more people to see your resource and getting more impressions, that help the algorithm notice that the post is cracked basically. The problem lately in X is that some lead magnet posts that request for a comment or a rt are nerfed by the algorithm. So, for now x articles seem like the way to go, but they still look really good in linkedin.

After they comment, they get sent an automatic dm with the resource.

After they get the resource, we usually check their profile, if you can gather that they are qualified, you try to spark a convo without being salesy, just ask them what they thought about the resource. If they reply you have a convo going, and from there you just ask what they're working on right now. That's the qualifying part and it doesn't feel like qualifying.

Then if they're a fit you don't pitch on that message, you keep it going until they say something that opens the door, usually a problem they bring up themselves. That's when the call comes up and it lands soft because they said it first.

One thing to be honest about, right now the dm links straight to the notion page, there's no landing page and no email capture. So we don't own the contact, if they never reply the lead is just gone. That's the biggest hole in the machine and I'd rather say it than pretend the chain is finished.

---

**2. If you had to split the result across the parts, the post, the asset, the DM, the landing page, the P.S. offer, what's the real percentage each one contributes?**

This isn't measured so it's my gut, don't quote it as data.

The post is basically half of it. If the post doesn't pull comments then nothing else in the machine even runs, and a beautiful asset behind a dead post does literally nothing.

The dm is next, maybe a quarter. It's the only part that turns a claim into a conversation and most people waste it by just dropping the link and going quiet.

The asset itself I'd say 15%. It has to be good enough that they don't feel tricked, and after that the extra polish doesn't really buy you anything.

The landing page and the P.S. offer are the rest, and for us they're close to zero right now because we haven't built the landing page and the P.S. still isn't locked.

The funny part is the asset is the thing everyone spends all their time on and it's third on that list.

---

**3. What's the single most common thing agency owners get wrong when they decide "I need a lead magnet"?**

They treat it like a thing to make instead of a thing to run. So they spend two weeks on a really nice pdf, post it once, get like eleven comments, and decide lead magnets don't work.

The other one is the title. They name a feeling instead of naming a thing. "How to fix your creative testing" gets nothing, "creative testing checklist" gets comments, because one of them names something that actually lands in your dms and the other one is just a topic.

---

**4. When did it click for you that the asset was the least important part of the whole thing?**

It clicked when I started to test the same lead magnet resource on different post copy. They were going to get the same thing, the exact same resource, but I just placed a different copy and a different image or video that they were seeing, and the PERCEIVED VALUE increased.

That's the whole thing right there. The resource never changed. What changed was what they thought they were getting before they got it. So if the same asset can do nothing or do really well depending on what's wrapped around it, then the asset is obviously not the part doing the work.

---

**5. What breaks most often in the machine, and how do you know it's broken vs just a slow week?**

The post breaks most often. Everything else is fine and the post just doesn't pull, and then people go and rewrite the asset, which fixes nothing.

The way you tell is comment rate, not comment count. If the post got normal reach and almost nobody commented, that's the title or the promise in the copy. If the reach itself died, that's distribution and the magnet had nothing to do with it. Two completely different problems and people mix them up all the time.

The other one that breaks quietly is fatigue. Same magnet, same audience, and the redemptions just fall off. On the agency's account we watched one go from 7,839 down to 901 across five dms in six days. Nothing was wrong with it, it was just spent. So you rest a magnet 8 to 12 weeks before running it again.

---

**6. What's the one leading indicator you watch to know a magnet is actually working before any calls get booked?**

Comment rate on the post, comments divided by views.

Everything else is way too slow. Booked calls take weeks and by then you can't tell which post did it. Comment rate tells you on day one if the title and the promise landed, and that's the part you'd actually go change.

If you want a quality signal instead of a volume one, look at how many of the people who claimed it actually reply to the dm. Lots of claims and no replies means you pulled the wrong crowd, which is worse than pulling fewer.

---

**7. How is the way you run this different from the "post a freebie, hope for comments" version everyone else runs?**

The freebie version stops at the asset. You post it, people comment, they get a link, done. Nobody ever looks at who claimed it.

What makes it a machine is what happens after delivery. We check the profile, we decide if they're qualified, and we start a conversation with the ones who are. That's a human step and it's the one nobody builds because it doesn't feel like a system.

The other difference is it's built to run again. Same skill, same templates, new magnet, every week. Most people build one and treat it like a launch.

---

**8. What does a magnet that converts to pipeline have that a magnet that just gets likes doesn't?**

Something they have to fill in.

If there's nothing in it they complete themselves they just skim it and close it and you never hear from them again. If page three makes them write their own answers, they've done work inside your thing, and the dm after that lands completely differently.

The other test is whether the title names an artifact, a thing they can picture receiving. That's what makes typing the comment worth it.

---

**9. Tell me about a magnet that flopped. What part of the machine failed, and what did you change?**

A lot of them flopped, so I can't really tell you exactly which one. I've posted 3 lead magnets per week over the last 18 months, so yeah, there's a lot of them and most of the flops blur together.

What I can tell you is the pattern, it was almost always the post and almost never the resource. Same resource would do nothing one week and then do fine later with different copy on top of it, which is the same thing I said in question 4.

---

**10. If someone could only build one part of the machine well this week, which part and why?**

The post. It's half the result and it's the cheapest thing to fix.

If I had one afternoon I'd spend all of it on the title and the four lines under it, and I'd reuse an asset that already exists instead of building a new one.

---

**11. What number would you point to that proves this isn't theory for you?**

3 lead magnets a week for 18 months. That's the number. Most people have run one or two, I've run a couple hundred of these and watched what happens after each one.

The other one I'd point at is the comment rate table from the agency's account, 40 magnet posts. The named concrete magnets pulled around 4% comment rate against 0.9% for the broad AI one. Same account, same period, so it's a clean comparison and it's the whole title argument in one line.
