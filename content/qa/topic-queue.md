## Topic 2 — The DM is a quarter of the result and nobody builds it

**The flag:** the message that hands over the resource is the only part that turns a claim into a conversation, and most people drop a link there and go quiet.

**Why it's next:** Topic 1 ranked the DM second at about 25% and then moved on. The whole conversation-to-call sequence got four sentences. That's the biggest piece of unmined material in the file, and it pairs with `skills/dm-setting/industries/agency-owners/`.

**What it unlocks:** a short-form batch on setting, an X article on the post-delivery conversation, a YouTube board walking the real message-by-message sequence, and a possible lead magnet (the actual DM scripts) once Mauro signs off.

**Questions:**
1. Walk me through the auto-DM itself, word for word. What's in the message that delivers the resource, and what's deliberately left out?
2. You check the profile before you say anything. What are you actually looking for, and what disqualifies someone in five seconds?
3. What do you send after "what did you think of it?" gets a one-word reply?
4. How long do you let a conversation sit before you drop it? What does a dead lead look like?
5. What percentage of people who claim a magnet ever reply? Is that number measured or gut?
6. "You keep it going until they say something that opens the door." Give me three real examples of what that opening sounds like.
7. How do you move from their problem to the call in one message without it turning into a pitch?
8. What's the worst version of this you've sent? The message that killed the conversation.
9. What do you do differently when they're clearly qualified but they went quiet after the resource?
10. Which part of this is automated today, and which part will stay human on purpose?

**Clearance to watch:** reply rates and call-booked rates are unmeasured. If Mauro quotes any, mark them as gut in the file.

## Topic 3 — The title is doing the work

**Questions:**
1. Pull up the 40-post table. Read me the five best titles and the five worst, and tell me what separates them.
2. What's your actual process for naming a magnet? Do you write the title before or after the asset?
3. "Creative testing checklist" beat "how to fix your creative testing". Give me four more pairs from your own posts.
4. Is there a word or format that shows up in almost every winner? Checklist, swipe file, teardown, prompts, system?
5. When does a broad title beat a specific one? You said broad plus a magnet spirals more often, so where's the line?
6. How do the four lines under the title work? What's line one doing, what's line four doing?
7. What does the image or video on the post do that the copy can't?
8. Same asset, different title. How much did the comment rate actually move in your tests?
9. What titles have you tried that pulled comments from completely the wrong people?
10. If you had to hand an agency owner a naming rule they could apply this afternoon, what is it?
