# @shannholmberg — profile study

Steps 2, 3 and 4 of the four-step process. Step 1 raw capture: 52 items, 2026-08-11 to
2026-09-14, median 9,553 impressions. Numbers computed in `shannholmberg-2026-09-15-stats.py`.

**Limits.** One account, 35 days, n=52. Impressions are timeline figures read at one moment and
they keep climbing after. Topic labels are mine, not his, so the topic table is the softest thing
here. No follower count or follower delta was captured, so nothing below says whether reach
converts to follows.

---

## Step 2 · Success and outliers

**The concentration is steeper than 80/20.** Top 10 items carry 81% of the month's 2,318,220
impressions. Top 5 carry 59%. One item, the article, carries 11% on its own.

**Quote tweets are the account.**

| Type | n | share of items | median | outliers | share of reach |
|---|---:|---:|---:|---:|---:|
| quote tweet | 35 | 67% | 16,823 | 11 | 81% |
| post | 16 | 31% | 2,239 | 1 | 7% |
| article | 1 | 2% | 257,640 | 1 | 11% |

A quote tweet's median is 7.5x a post's. Eleven of the thirteen outliers are quote tweets.

**Carousel is the format that carries it.**

| Media | n | median | outliers | share of reach |
|---|---:|---:|---:|---:|
| carousel | 18 | 22,988 | 8 | 63% |
| image | 17 | 22,876 | 5 | 33% |
| none | 6 | 10,678 | 0 | 3% |
| video | 5 | 3,140 | 0 | 1% |
| link card | 6 | 682 | 0 | 0% |

**Video never breaks the median.** Five videos, zero outliers, 1% of reach. On an account this
size that is not noise.

**Text-only sits at the median and never above it.** Six items, median 10,678, zero outliers. The
answer to why a plain text post gets 13,000 views is that 13,000 is his floor, not his ceiling.

**Every call to action is a separate post.** Six link-card posts, median 682, all below median,
each one published within minutes of a high performer. The main post never carries the link.

## Step 3 · Why the account pops

Three engines, not one.

**Engine 1: he is the translation layer on other people's launches.**

Tool reactions are 15% of his items and 23% of his reach, with 4 outliers. Someone ships a thing,
and within about a day he posts "how to [marketing job] with [their thing]", numbered, as a
carousel.

Wayfinder, a grill-me skill, Hermes bot mode, an X Ads MCP, GPT-6 Astra. He is not the source of
any of that news. The launch carries the reach, and he supplies the marketing framing that the
launch itself does not have.

**Engine 2: stack lists borrow every tool's audience at once.**

Stack posts are 10% of his items and 35% of his reach. The single biggest item in the month,
546,619 impressions, is seven words of text over a carousel: "my AI stack has changed over the
past months".

That is the lowest-effort post in the set. It works because a list of named tools gives every one
of those tools a reason to amplify it.

**Engine 3: reference content is what makes the reach stick.**

Second brain, context and skills library are 24% of his items and 20% of his reach, and they hold
the highest save rates in the set.

The bookmark-to-like ratio separates the two kinds of winner cleanly. The 546,619 post sits at
0.67, the only top-eight item under 1.0. Every other big one runs 1.5 to 2.4, and the article hits
2.37. So the stack post is curiosity and the brain posts are reference, and they do different jobs.

**The combination is the answer.** Launches supply reach he did not have to earn. Stack lists
supply distribution from the tools he names. Reference posts give the arriving traffic a reason to
stay. Remove any one and the other two weaken.

**One more thing, on cadence.** 52 items in 35 days is about 1.5 a day, on one narrow subject.
Nothing in the set is off-topic except two personal posts, both of which landed under 2,000.

## Step 4 · The vehicles, templatized

**V1 · Tool reaction.** Someone ships. Quote it inside 24 hours. Title is "how to [job your ICP
does] with [their thing]". Numbered steps. Carousel. No link.

**V2 · Stack list.** "my [domain] stack", numbered, one line per tool, every tool tagged. Carousel.
The text can be one sentence.

**V3 · Layers breakdown.** "[thing] needs N layers", numbered, sub-bullets under each. Carousel.

**V4 · Reference piece.** How to organise [knowledge / context / process] so it can be used. This
is the save-me post and it builds the follow.

**V5 · The mega-article.** One in the month, 41,813 characters, 12 sections, 43 in-body images,
257,640 impressions. Eleven percent of a month's reach from one artifact.

**V6 · CTA follow-up.** A separate post, link card, published right after the main one. Expect
about 700 views and do not judge it on reach.

### What transfers to us, and what does not

**Transfers cleanly.** The CTA as a separate post. Carousel over video. Numbered breakdowns. One
narrow subject. A monthly mega-article.

**Transfers with a different calendar.** Engine 1 needs a launch calendar your ICP follows. Ours do
not track model releases, they track platform changes. Meta releases are our version of his tool
launches, and our own data already agrees: the Andromeda posts hit roughly 4x the comment rate of
anything else on the account.

**Does not transfer.** Engine 2. Naming AI tools borrows their audiences. Naming ad platforms
borrows nothing, because a platform does not amplify you.

## What this study is worth as content

A breakdown of a big account borrows its authority. Two forms work, the name and the number.
Running this on several accounts produces the number version, which is the stronger of the two.
