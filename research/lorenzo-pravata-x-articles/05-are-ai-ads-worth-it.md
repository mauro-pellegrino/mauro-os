## Article body

Half the brands that see our work ask the same question: is this AI?

The funny part is they ask it about the ads we shot in a real studio, with real people, on a real camera.

So before you decide whether AI ads are worth running, you have to understand what's actually going on here. Here's the honest answer.

### where the "AI ads look fake" fear comes from

The fear is real, and it's earned.

A lot of AI ads do look like AI.

Not because the tool is bad, but because of how people use it. They open a blank prompt, type "write me a winning ad for my supplement," and post whatever comes out. You can feel it instantly.

*[An embedded quote-post appears to have been stripped from the source paste here. The next line, "He's right", has no antecedent in the pasted text.]*

He's right. People point AI at a blank page and expect strategy to come out the other end. It doesn't work like that. If that's what "AI ads" means to you, of course they're not worth it.

### AI isn't the problem

We get asked if our studio ads are AI.

Real actors, real conversations, real footage, and people still squint and ask.

The weirdness around AI has gotten so loud that "is this AI?" has become a reflex, even when the answer is no.

And it doesn't matter.

Your customer scrolling at 11pm is not going to analyze 100% on whether your ad was filmed or generated.

They stop or they don't.

They buy or they don't.

Meta delivers your ad based on how people behave, not on which software made it.

The whole "is it AI?" fight is a distraction from the only question that pays the bills: is this creative different enough to win.

### why we use AI anyway, and love it

AI is the easiest way we've ever had to find creative diversity.

After Andromeda, the game is volume of genuinely different creative.

You need 50+ concepts a month with different hooks that actually look and feel different from each other, or the algorithm flattens them and your account stalls.

No team produces that by hand at a sane cost.

AI lets us spin up angles, scripts, and concepts fast, then push them into testing and let the data tell us what's working.

That's the part it's built for, and it's changed how much ground we can cover in a week.

The trap is using it for sameness.

Take one concept, have AI reword the hook 50 times, and you get 50 ads with the same fingerprint that Meta feeds to the same people.

### we run both, and so should you

Studio and AI do different jobs for us, and we use on both.

Studio is where we build the premium, brand-defining work.

Podcast clips, VSL interviews, founder stories, the formats that don't look like ads and earn trust before they ever sell.

The ones people can't believe are real.

AI is where we get range.

Fast angles, fast variations, fast testing into whatever format is winning that week.

With great scriptwriting.

Run together, they feed the algorithm exactly what it wants right now: a steady stream of creative that's high quality and genuinely different.

If you want creative that converts and nobody can tell how it was made, studio, AI, or both, DM me "AI" and I'll show you what we'd run for your account.

## Structural skeleton (the part that ports)

1. **Open on a question the reader is already asking**, then immediately invert it. Everyone asks "is this AI?" about the footage that is least AI. The inversion is the hook, and it's a real anecdote rather than a constructed reframe.
2. **Concede the objection fully before answering it.** "The fear is real, and it's earned. A lot of AI ads do look like AI." No defensiveness. The concession is what buys permission for the rest.
3. **Relocate the blame from the tool to the operator.** People point AI at a blank page and expect strategy out. This is belief 4 almost word for word (AI is leverage inside a workflow, not a replacement for taste).
4. **Dismiss the debate as economically irrelevant.** "Your customer scrolling at 11pm is not going to analyze whether your ad was filmed or generated. They stop or they don't." Cuts the culture-war framing and replaces it with a commercial one.
5. **Then flip to the pro case**, so the article isn't an anti-AI or pro-AI take. It's a *how to use it* take wearing a debate's clothes.
6. **Name the trap** (using AI for sameness, 50 rewordings with the same fingerprint), which is the same volume-without-differentiation point from articles 01 and 04 in a third costume.
7. **Both/and resolution with a job assigned to each.** Studio = premium and trust. AI = range and speed. Neither replaces the other.
8. **DM keyword "AI"**, same diagnosis-not-asset pattern as 02, 03 and 04.

**What separates this one from the other four:** it's a position piece. There is no pipeline, no lever list, no swipe file. The value is a stance, and the proof is an anecdote (people ask if our real footage is AI) rather than a metric.
