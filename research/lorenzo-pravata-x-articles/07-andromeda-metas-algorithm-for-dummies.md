## Article body

There's a Reddit thread going around with 80+ comments, all calling Meta's Andromeda update the same thing: a scam, a downgrade, a money grab.

They agree something broke. They're all wrong about what.

I've watched Andromeda hit real accounts spending past $1M a month, not just read the threads.

### what andromeda actually does

Forget the chips and the machine learning papers.

Before Andromeda, Meta read you shallow. You clicked a shoe, you liked shoe content, Meta filed you under "likes shoes."

After Andromeda, Meta reads you deep.

1. The color that makes you stop scrolling.
2. The design you linger on.
3. The hour you actually buy.
4. Whether you finish a video or thumb past it
5. Whether emotional content moves you or a straight product demo does.

Look at how much of that is visual.

The color, the design, the thing that makes you stop.

Meta is reading your creative with its eyes before it reads a single word of your copy.

Then it does the part that matters.

It takes that full picture of how you behave and matches it to a specific creative.

Not your adset. Your creative.

That's the whole change.

Targeting used to live in the adset. Now it lives inside the ad itself. The creative decides who sees it.

This is why everyone on your feed is telling you to collapse your adsets and pile creatives into one campaign.

The advice is right.

The reason they give for it is usually wrong...

### the contradiction everyone's stuck on

Walk through the comments on any Andromeda post and one objection keeps coming back.

If Meta wants more creative variations but fewer adsets, isn't that a contradiction?

More creatives always meant more adsets.

How do you do one without the other?

Here's the piece they're missing:

Every creative gets its own entity ID, think of it like a fingerprint.

If your creatives are genuinely different, they get different fingerprints, and Meta delivers each one to a different type of buyer. If they're not, they get the same fingerprint, Meta treats them as one ad, and they fight each other for the same audience.

And here's the part nobody in that thread says out loud.

Different means it has to look different.

A new hook on the same template is not a new ad.

Same background, same framing, same face, reworded text on top, and Meta fingerprints it as the same thing.

Visually different is what earns a different fingerprint. A podcast clip next to a claymation ad next to a founder talking straight into his phone.

Different setting, different format, different person.

So the contradiction goes away the second you get it. You don't need more adsets. You need creatives that actually look different from each other, inside fewer adsets.

That's exactly where most accounts fall apart.

### Check the complaints again

The founder asking how a small business with no budget and no time to film is supposed to keep up.

The one calling it a failure because the old system was "faster and cleaner."

The apparel guy saying remixing one tee 20 ways doesn't build brand trust.

Take the words away and it's the same complaint every single time.

The algorithm now rewards creative volume they can't produce.

And the apparel founder is the most right of all of them, completely by accident.

Remixing one tee 20 ways doesn't build trust because it was never 20 ads.

It's one shot with the color swapped 20 times, and Meta sees straight through it.

Real diversity is 20 genuinely different pieces, different angles, formats, and faces, that still clearly feel like your brand.

Andromeda took the lever out of the media buyer's hands.

The only thing that moves it is whether you can feed it a steady supply of creatives that actually look different from each other.

For a founder spending $50k+ a month, that's the entire game now.

### so is it helping you or hurting you

If you can put out 50+ genuinely different creatives a month, across formats, angles, and awareness levels, Andromeda is the best update Meta has shipped in years.

It finds your winners faster and it protects them longer.

If you can't, it's going to keep feeling like a scam, because the accounts that can produce are quietly eating the audiences you used to reach for free.

## Structural skeleton (the part that ports)

1. **Open on a public argument, then declare everyone in it wrong.** A Reddit thread with 80+ comments is a free, verifiable, borrowed conflict. The reader doesn't have to take the writer's word that the debate exists.
2. **"They agree something broke. They're all wrong about what."** The whole article in eight words. It concedes the premise and rejects the diagnosis, which is a stronger position than either agreeing or disagreeing.
3. **Experience over commentary, stated once, plainly.** "I've watched Andromeda hit real accounts spending past $1M a month, not just read the threads." One line separating the author from every other voice in the debate.
4. **Explain the mechanism in plain words and refuse the jargon out loud** ("forget the chips and the machine learning papers"). Then a numbered list of what the algorithm reads, followed by an observation about the list itself ("look at how much of that is visual"). Making the reader notice the pattern lands harder than stating it.
5. **Name the reader's confusion as a named section** ("the contradiction everyone's stuck on"). The article argues *with* the audience's actual objection instead of a strawman.
6. **One piece of hard, checkable IP:** entity ID as a fingerprint, and the claim that visual sameness collapses fingerprints. This is the thing the reader can't get from the thread.
7. **Re-read the complaints through the new frame.** Three real objections, restated, then collapsed into one root cause. Then the generous move: the apparel founder is "the most right of all of them, completely by accident." Crediting an opponent while correcting them is more persuasive than dunking.
8. **A two-branch verdict.** If you can produce, it's the best update in years. If you can't, it'll keep feeling like a scam. The reader self-sorts, and the branch they land in is the sales argument.
9. **Proof placed after the argument, not before** ($50k/mo → $1M/mo). By that point the reader has accepted the mechanism, so the result reads as confirmation rather than a flex.
10. **CTA with a diagnostic trigger:** "if your top spenders are already 3+ months old". A specific, checkable symptom, not a generic "if you want help".

**This is the most sophisticated argument structure in the set.** It's the only article that runs on a borrowed public conflict, and the only one that wins by re-interpreting other people's words rather than presenting its own material.
