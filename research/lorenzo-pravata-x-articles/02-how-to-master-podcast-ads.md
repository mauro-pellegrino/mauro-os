## Article body

We've generated over $106M for our clients running stealth creatives on Meta.

These are ads that don't look like ads, like our podcast ads.

Here's exactly how we build them, and why they keep outperforming everything else in our clients' ad account.

### Why they work

Cold traffic is the hardest audience to convert:

- They don't know your product
- They don't know exactly what problem they have

And the second they see an ad, if it's not relevant to them, they will probably scroll.

Podcast ads disarm that reflex they get.

When a viewer lands on what looks like a podcast clip, a format they already consume and trust, they don't apply the "is this trying to sell me something?" filter.

If they script is good enough, they watch and will listen.

That's why we consistently hit 40-45% hook rates with this format

When most agencies struggle to crack 30.

And the key mechanic inside every podcast ad that actually scales: the listener reaction line.

This is the moment where the second person says "Wait, what do you mean?" or "That sounds expensive" or "No way."

It sounds like it doesn't make a difference, but its doing almost all the persuasion work.

Without it, you have a monologue with a second voice added on top.

Which is what most brands and agencies settle for.

They try a bad podcast ad and just cut off all takes for their product.

One script we ran for our client opened with:

"Your t-shirt looks so good. How long have you been working out?"

That ad spent over $100k.

The viewer never heard a product pitch in the first 20 seconds.

They heard a real conversation and wanted to know where it went.

### 6 hooks that are proven

After going through winning scripts across 50+ brands in different categories, 6 opening structures keep appearing at the top for us.

**"I just found something..."**

Starts with a discovery, one person tells another they found something surprising.
Curiosity-pull before any product context.
Works for cold audiences who have no idea they have the problem yet.

**"It blows my mind that X are still using Y"**

Contrarian opener.
Challenges an assumption the viewer already holds about ANY industry.
Frames the current behaviour as the problem before the product appears.

**"So you're telling me..."**

Opens mid-conversation, as if the viewer walked in on something already in progress.
The viewer WILL want to listen to the rest if you actually do your research and fit some quality reviews into your briefs. That will help your scriptwriting, plus, now with AI, there's no excuses.

**"They say it takes (common industry assumption)"**

Sticker shock hook.
Names the conventional wisdom, then questions it.
"They say it takes 6 months to learn Spanish." for example was a big one for us. Our language learning app client scaled like crazy with an ad that opened like that.

**"Haven't you noticed..."**

Pattern recognition hook.
Points to something the viewer has observed but never articulated. "Haven't you noticed that some people in their 50s just look different?"
Viewer thinks: yes, actually.
The conversation explains why.

**"Your [result] is so good..."**

A compliment on a result the other person achieved. Immediately creates aspiration. The conversation is the answer to how they got to that, since that's how normal conversations get structured.

Each structure we use internally works for a different awareness level.

"I just found..." is for unaware audiences who need to understand the problem before the product makes sense.

"Your result is so good..." is for people who already know change is possible.

### Production

The script can be perfect and the ad still fails if the production gives it away.

We shoot in our studios, with actors from whichever country our client is from.

- English
- Spanish
- Italian

The setup is built around one goal: make it look like a clip from a real podcast that happened to go viral.

Two cameras is the standard. 50-85mm lenses, F2.8 to F4, actors positioned well away from the background so you get natural depth.

When you cut between angles it feels like watching Lex Fridman clips on your feed, not a sponsored post.

Actor positioning matters more than most people expect.

The 45 degree angle is the setup that works.

### Some details about actors

They need to be spontaneous.

If they look like they're recalling what comes next, the ad is unusable.

We brief them on the persona, not the product.

Their job is to be two real people in a real conversation.

The product is the conclusion of that conversation, not the reason they're sitting there.

The signal everything worked for us is when we were getting comments on the ad asking which podcast the clip is from.

A big one we are receiving recently is people asking if the podcast is AI haha.

### the mistake most brands fall to

The mistake we see consistently is jumping from product introduction straight to CTA.

The feature stack, the objection handling, the risk reversal exist for one reason: to let the viewer sell themselves.

By the time the CTA arrives, the decision is already made.

They just need permission to act on it.
