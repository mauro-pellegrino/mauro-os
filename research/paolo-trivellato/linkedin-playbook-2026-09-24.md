## 1. Profile visits is the metric, not impressions

> *"The number one metric that we've found directly tied to the amount of calls we booked... it's
> profile visits. Every time there's a spike in the profile visits is when he gets more booked
> calls."*

Over a year of working with the same client, profile visits is the only leading indicator he
trusts. Impressions matter because they drive profile visits, not on their own.

**The thresholds he gave:**

| Impressions a month | Profile visits a month | What to do |
|---|---|---|
| under 200K | under 2K | Broad lead magnets. Nothing else |
| 200K to 400K | 2K to 3K | Add 2 bottom-of-funnel posts a week |

**Lead magnets break the relationship between the two**, and that is the point. A lead magnet post
gets fewer impressions than a normal post and far more profile visits, because everyone who wants
the thing goes to look at you. So track profile visits and let impressions be whatever they are.

## 2. The capture funnel, which is the part most people skip

His client's flow, end to end:

1. Post goes out with the CTA **in the image**, never in the copy
2. Lead Shark replies to every comment with an opt-in link
3. Opt-in page takes an email
4. Redirect to a **thank-you page with a VSL**, case studies and a book-a-call
5. Email arrives with the resource
6. Short sequence follows

**Why reply-to-comment beats a DM.** You lose the back-and-forth, and you gain the lurkers. People
who will not comment publicly can still click the link in someone else's comment thread.

**The 3-email sequence, 3 days:**

1. The resource, plus a line saying more is coming this week, plus a soft CTA. Reply with a word,
   or book a call
2. A case study
3. A direct CTA

> *"That's already going to get you the bulk of the results."*

**The weekly newsletter is the least important piece.** He said so twice. The VSL page and the
short sequence are what matter.

**Where the bookings actually land:** the profile, then the VSL page. People see the content, see
the email, then go to the profile and book from there. The newsletter rarely gets direct credit.

## 3. Lead magnets

**Cadence:** one a day is the ceiling and it is fine to run it. LinkedIn does not punish
back-to-back winners the way X does. Once you hit 2K profile visits a month, dial down to about 5
a week and add bottom-of-funnel.

**The image is the most important part of the post.** Not the copy.

> *"If you can make it as complicated as possible, it's kind of gonna work better... the hover
> time on that is just gonna work. They're gonna zoom in."*

Dense, complex infographics. His client got 600+ and 1,000+ comments on these.

**The Notion-document image style is dead.** He ran it two years ago and it worked then.
> *"I haven't seen this format do well in..."*

**Never put the CTA in the post copy.** This is a recent algorithm change and it is absolute.

> *"If you say to comment a word in the post copy, it's going to get killed."*

Put it in the image, or in a banner on the video.

**Delivery format:** always Notion. Every resource, including Claude skills. Easiest to consume
and there is no reason to use anything else.

## 4. Where ideas come from, and they are 90% of the work

> *"I think ideas is like 90% of it now."*

Copy quality matters less than he expected. He sees posts with obviously-AI copy go viral. The
skill is in picking what to write about.

**Trend-jack from X.** X runs days to weeks ahead of LinkedIn on anything AI. When a new tool or
model ships, the X crowd is on it immediately. Be the first to bring that use case to LinkedIn.

> *"LinkedIn... they love novelty."*

**The swipe file, and the scoring that makes it work:**

- Track a set of profiles on X and LinkedIn
- For each account, compute its own average
- Score every post against **that account's average**, not against raw numbers
- A 10x outlier from a small account beats a big number from a huge one

> *"Somebody with 100,000 followers that gets 100 likes is probably actually an underperformance."*

Same logic as a YouTube outlier score.

**Baselines for what is worth copying:** X, at least 50K impressions, better over 100K. LinkedIn,
300 to 400 comments minimum.

**Manual search still matters.** Cycle 5 to 10 keywords on X to catch accounts that are popping off
and are not on the list yet.

**How to copy:** take the structure and the design, change the angle, the topic and the colours.
Feed the image to Codex or Claude and say use this as a template.

> *"It is my client, but I don't care. People are going to copy anyone."*

## 5. Broad versus ICP content

**70/30.** 70% broad for volume, 30% hyper-specific for the people who will actually buy.

The specific 30% performs about 10x worse on reach and converts far better. Name the niche, the
problem and the numbers, then put a direct book-a-call CTA on it.

**Order matters.** Broad first to build the volume, then add the specific once there is a
conversion mechanism behind it.

**Case-study lead magnets are his best converting format**, because a case study positioned as a
valuable breakdown escapes the thing that normally kills case studies.

> *"Traditionally case studies, nobody wants to listen to them. You're just selling. But if you
> position it as a valuable breakdown where you're showing people how you achieved that result,
> you're able to get tens of thousands of impressions on a case study, which would never happen
> before."*

## 6. Outbound to people who already engaged

This is the part he has only recently implemented and is already seeing calls from.

**The export trick.** In Lead Shark, export from the **post** section, not the leads section. The
leads export drops the data you need: which post they commented on, how many posts they engaged
with.

**Then filter on two things:**

1. ICP fit, scored by Claude
2. Engagement count. Three or more posts engaged with means they are deep in the funnel

**The message.** Do not over-personalise.

> *"The biggest thing with outbound is just having a good lead list. If you're reaching out to
> people at the right time, that's just what works."*

His actual structure: mention they engaged a couple of times, reference the most recent comment if
it says anything, one case study, then *"open to seeing how I'd approach it?"*

His own campaign on this shape runs about a **20% interested-reply rate from accepted
connections.**

**Volume.** Start with 5 prospects a week and have a human own it.

## 7. Cold LinkedIn outbound, if you want it later

He rents 10 LinkedIn accounts for about **$1,300 a month**, runs **15 connections a day per
account**, so 150 a day.

His list is funded SaaS companies and the hook is one well-known case study. That is the whole
campaign.

**His advice was not to start here.** The lower-hanging fruit is always: more impressions, then
warm outbound to engaged ICP, then cold.

## 8. Multiple profiles

Second and third profiles work, and a fresh account often outperforms an established one.

> *"There's definitely something where having a new account, a lot of the time, just
> fundamentally performs better."*

His client's second profile was at times outperforming the main one and bringing more qualified
calls. One agency he worked with posted across 10 accounts, though that was a $99/month mass-market
SaaS.

**Do the capture funnel first.** More profiles multiply whatever conversion rate already exists.

## 9. X, from the same call

**Add one mid-to-long-form listicle post a day.** Pillars of a system, or "the 19 things I'd
systemise if I wanted to get a new channel to $1M in 6 months".

Why it works: once someone clicks see more, it takes a while to scroll past, so dwell time goes up
and bookmarks go up.

**Articles breaking down a big company's strategy.** His Clay GTM breakdown did **300K impressions**
and Clay themselves commented asking him to run their account.

> *"I closed multiple 8-figure agencies and bigger companies because these are the types of people
> that they look up to. Nobody making $10K a month is really going to care about what a
> billion-dollar company is doing."*

The subject qualifies the reader. Break down a company your target buyer admires and you attract
people at that level.

**Auto-DMs on X:** he stopped, but only because he got caught in a ban wave aimed at crypto
giveaway accounts. He believes they still work and knows people running them.

## 10. Tooling

| Tool | What for |
|---|---|
| **Lead Shark** | comment replies, DM automation, the post-level export |
| **HeyReach** or **Gojiberry** | separate outbound, away from the main account |
| **Claude / Codex** | ICP scoring, swipe-file filtering, image templating |
| **Notion** | every resource, always |

**Where templates live:** in a doc or a board, **not inside the Claude skills.** Formats change
constantly. The skill holds the repeatable stuff, removing AI tells and basic copywriting.

## What he could not answer

**Attributing a booked call to a specific post.** He tracks lead-magnet source, profile, email and
VSL opt-in, and accepts that the post-level link is missing.

> *"The only thing that we can't really track is the direct qualified calls booked."*

His workaround is a separate Calendly link per source, and asking people on the call where they
found you.

## The two things worth arguing with

**The image claim.** He says the image is the most important part of a lead-magnet post and that
more complex is better. That is his LinkedIn-specialist view on a recent algorithm. It directly
contradicts what other advisors say about visuals. It is probably right for lead magnets
specifically and it is not a general rule.

**His numbers are from a B2B SaaS and agency audience.** When asked whether ecom is harder on
LinkedIn he said yes, and gave the reason:

> *"With you guys, probably 90% of your comments are other agencies trying to steal the playbook."*

That is a real structural limit on any account selling to ecom operators on LinkedIn, and it should
be priced in before setting targets.
