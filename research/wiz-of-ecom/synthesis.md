## What is actually usable as content

Ranked by fit with the ICP (established agency owners) and Mauro's lane (inbound, AI content systems that book calls).

**1. The highest-ROI part of an AI content engine has no AI in it.**
Manual CRM follow-up on every past qualified lead who did not close or did not show. No sequences, no automation, a human working the stages: showed, interested, ready to buy soon, contract sent, ghosted. The reasoning is that every journey diverges, so a sequence guesses. For the guy positioned as the AI operator, this is the strongest contrarian piece in the whole call, and it is belief 4 stated as a system. Vehicle: long-form or YouTube.

**2. Content is not the bottleneck. Capture and convert are.**
The sequencing is content, then capture, then convert. Agency owners burn a year making more content when the leak sits between the post and the booked call. Capture is the lead magnet with name, email and phone, a thank-you page, a welcome flow, 4-7 emails a week. Convert is the CRM and someone calling immediately. Flagship candidate: this is a full YouTube video and an article.

**3. Auto-DMs are dead and it was not gradual.**
X banned around 40,000 accounts for automation, and anything auto-DM shaped stopped converting after that. Mauro was running 1-2 a day and pulled them. First-hand, dated, specific, and it kills a tactic a chunk of the ICP is still paying for. Vehicle: short value tweet, then a doc if it wins.

**4. The reach drop was a vehicle problem, not an algorithm problem.**
Mauro saw a drop from 1 August. Operators running different formats had their best month. That reframes "the algorithm hates me" into "your formats aged out and you did not notice". Strong opener against the "I post and get nothing back" pain.

**5. The pre-created strategy as the close.**
For a qualified lead who went quiet: build them a content strategy plus three finished content pieces, with the objection they actually raised on the call answered inside it. Pull the objection by running the call recordings and transcripts through AI. This is Mauro's lane exactly, it is AI as leverage rather than replacement, and in a crowded market demonstrated competency beats claimed competency. Candidate for a skill, not just a post.

**6. Promotion as an episode, not a pitch.**
Instead of a three-part sales post, narrate ongoing wins ("wins from the last 24 hours", then quote it forward with 15 wins). The account reads as a running story rather than a sales cycle. This is the direct answer to the ICP's cringe fear, which is pillar 1.

**7. Low likes, high-quality comments, booked calls.**
A behind-the-scenes agency update pulled 6-7 likes and 25 comments from large accounts, every one replied to, and it produced booked calls. Kills the likes-as-scoreboard belief with a mechanism instead of a platitude.

**8. Quote-tweet the flex with the how-to.**
When someone posts a viral AI creative or AI build, quote it with the actual build. X pushes the quote to everyone who engaged with the original, so the audience arrives pre-qualified and already curious. Mauro's version stays in his lane: quote the viral AI-content or agent flex with the real workflow, never ecom creative production.

**9. LinkedIn is the inverse of X.**
Fewer posts, around 5 a week. No infographics, no doc screenshots, no Reddit-style posts. The one visual that works is a screenshot of a viral X conversation you were already in, plus commentary. Threads become long-form with spacing. The claim on the record: the number one performer on X is the worst performer on LinkedIn.

**10. Keyword tracking inside posts.**
Keep a list of around 25 topical keywords, place them deliberately, track which ones the account wins with, and repeat those. Measurable, automatable, and nobody in the ICP is doing it.

**11. Theme pages are a sponsorship business, not a lead channel.**
On his own numbers, a theme page produced one lead in five months and it did not close, while sponsorships on it are near-pure profit. Useful for the agency owner about to build a faceless AI page expecting clients from it. Numbers stay his.

**12. The weekly review is the whole engine.**
Every Friday the team fills a control panel: booked calls by channel, sales by channel, follow-ups to close, close rate, show rate, website clicks by source, impressions, link clicks, pieces published. Sunday, three to four hours, one person reads it and sets the next seven days of tasks and vehicles. Mauro already runs a Monday acquisition analysis, so this is confirmation plus an upgrade to the input list.

**13. Honest lead magnet volume.**
Roughly 12-20 sign-ups per promoting post, and not all of them qualified. Worth saying out loud because the guru version never does.
