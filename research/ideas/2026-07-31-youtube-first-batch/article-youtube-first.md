We started on X. Moved to LinkedIn about a year and a half ago. Now we're going hard on YouTube and putting almost everything there first.

The logic isn't obvious, so I want to lay it out in a way you can copy for your own agency.

**the trickle-down**

Pick one platform to be great at. Make it YouTube.

Going from a good YouTube video to a good LinkedIn post, a good X thread, a good Instagram clip is easy. Going the other way is not.

A 15-minute video is the widest, deepest version of an idea you'll ever record. Once it exists, every other format is just a smaller cut of something that already works.

If I put all my effort into the quality of one YouTube video, I can make quality content everywhere else without starting from a blank page. The video is the source. Everything downstream inherits its quality.

Big to small is repurposing. Going small to big means inventing from nothing.

Take a LinkedIn post and try to expand it into a 15-minute video. There's nothing there to pull from. So the order you build in decides whether the rest of your week is easy or a scramble.

**why youtube wins for high-ticket**

We close $10k+ deals. At that price the whole game is trust.

Nobody wires you $10,000 off one post. They do it after watching you think out loud for 12 minutes about a topic you clearly know cold, until they feel like they already know how you work.

One article on LinkedIn or X can't carry that. It's one idea, read in ten seconds, then gone.

A 12-minute video is a different level entirely. The prospect sees your face, hears how you reason, watches you handle the messy parts. By the time they book a call they're half-sold, because they've spent more time with you than most of your competitors' prospects spend with anyone.

For a cheap product the post is enough. For a $10k agency retainer, you need the video.

**the part that makes it possible: ai**

Here's the honest problem. Two to three high-quality YouTube videos a week is a lot of work. Titles, scripts, competitor research, then repurposing across four platforms. That used to take a whole team.

This is where AI changed the decision, not just the execution.

The first thing I did when I started using Claude Code was build skills for the YouTube side of the machine. Idea generation. Title writing. Scriptwriting. Pulling what competitors are testing. I got the YouTube MCP running inside Claude Code and saved every skill natively on my computer.

Now the system does the heavy lifting and I bring the taste.

One thing to be honest about: the skill is bad at first. Sometimes the first version is genuinely terrible. And even when you think it's good, it can usually get a lot better.

So the framework is simple. Build a skill for one job, like scriptwriting. Test it on a real script, not a fake one. Read the output, find where it breaks, and ask Claude to edit the skill. Then run it again.

The point is you spend your hours building the machine that writes the content. Then the machine produces, and you keep sharpening it with real work every week. That's the difference between AI as a toy and AI as leverage inside a workflow you already run.

**the shape of the system**

So the whole thing looks like this.

One high-quality YouTube video is the source. Two to three of those a week, mapped out in advance so it's a system, not a scramble. AI skills handle the ideas, titles, scripts, research, and the repurposing down to X, LinkedIn, and Instagram. I stay in the loop on taste and the parts only I can say.

The output is a content engine fast enough to actually run at that volume, and consistent enough to produce real inbound instead of noise.

If your content is scattered across three platforms with nothing tying it together, flip the order. Go YouTube-first and let it trickle down.
