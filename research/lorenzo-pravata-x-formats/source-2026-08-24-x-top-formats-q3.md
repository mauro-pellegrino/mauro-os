# The 8 Content Formats That Actually Book Calls On X

*One quarter of first-party data. June 4 to August 24, 2026. 521 posts, 1,423,324 impressions, 1,734 link clicks, 107 DM conversations opened. Every format below has real posts attached so you can see it running.*

---

## Who this is for

Anyone running an X account who wants it to produce calls instead of applause.

You post consistently, the numbers look fine, and none of it turns into pipeline. That's usually not a volume problem or a writing problem. It's format. There are about eight content formats that do useful work on this platform, they do completely different jobs, and the two that produce almost all the clicks and conversations are the two most people never run.

I pulled a full quarter of the export to work out which format does what, and I've attached the actual posts to each one. What we sell is beside the point here, swap the subject for whatever you do and the structure holds.

One number before we start. Across the quarter we got 1,734 link clicks, and 90.5% of them came from a single format that accounted for 11.6% of our reach. That format is first on the list because nothing else in the data comes close.

---

## 1. Replying to your own post with the offer

163 posts in the quarter were replies sitting under our own content, carrying a link or an offer. They averaged 1,015 impressions each, roughly a third of account average. Save rate of 0.19%, basically nothing. 126 replies across all 163, so almost no engagement.

They produced 1,570 of our 1,734 link clicks. **90.5% of every click the account got, from 11.6% of its reach.**

Read that twice. The worst-performing format on every dashboard metric is the format producing nearly all the clicks. If you sorted your posts by engagement and cut the bottom, this is the first thing you'd delete.

Why it works is structural. A post carrying a link gets less distribution than a post without one, so you let the post earn its reach clean and hang the ask underneath once it's already moving. The parent gets scored, the reply rides on that reach, and anyone reading far enough down to see it has self-selected as interested.

**Examples, sorted by clicks:**

- [Jul 1](https://x.com/lorenzo_pravata/status/2072385886461853732) — 1,301 impressions, **86 clicks** (6.6% click rate)
- [Jun 14](https://x.com/lorenzo_pravata/status/2066241901829705933) — 2,267 impressions, **74 clicks**
- [Jul 24](https://x.com/lorenzo_pravata/status/2080750033029197957) — 867 impressions, **67 clicks** (7.7% click rate, best of the quarter)
- [Jun 5](https://x.com/lorenzo_pravata/status/2062989903445586266) — 2,551 impressions, **61 clicks**
- [Jun 11](https://x.com/lorenzo_pravata/status/2065168416219046194) — 1,814 impressions, **55 clicks**

Look at what those five have in common. None of them ask for a call. Every one announces that a body of work has been updated and points at it. Telling people the work exists beat asking them for the meeting, consistently, and the highest click rate of the whole quarter came off the smallest reach on that list.

```
Reply 1: [Name the mechanism visible in the post above]
Reply 2: [The structure, or the how, in two lines]
Reply 3: [The ask, framed as access to work that already exists, not as a booking request]
```

Three fixes if you're already doing some version of this. Run it under every post that gets traction, not the occasional one. Lead with the work rather than your calendar. And build the stack to two or three replies so there's something worth reading on the way down.

## 2. Breaking down someone else's operation

Nine posts in the quarter took apart a company the audience already knew. They averaged 15,160 impressions each, the highest average of any format we ran, against an account baseline of 2,731.

It works because it's verifiable. Anything you say about your own results, the reader has to decide whether to believe. Anything you say about a company they recognise, they can check in thirty seconds, and when it checks out you've built authority without making one claim about yourself. Much cheaper way to be credible than a case study.

**Examples:**

- [Jun 5](https://x.com/lorenzo_pravata/status/2062988614661750924) — **45,080 impressions**, 292 saves, 253 replies, 46 follows. Biggest post of the quarter. Frame was an unknown brand quietly out-performing a name everyone knows.
- [Jun 19](https://x.com/lorenzo_pravata/status/2067914366536613918) — 35,074 impressions, 226 saves, 40 profile visits
- [Jul 18](https://x.com/lorenzo_pravata/status/2078611205313151366) — 18,245 impressions, 263 replies, 160 profile visits, 35 follows
- [Jun 10](https://x.com/lorenzo_pravata/status/2064698163441586675) — 17,312 impressions, 242 saves

```
[Company they've heard of] + [a hard number proving you actually looked]
   → [The surprising thing you found]
   → [What they're doing, described in their language rather than yours]
   → [The principle underneath it, stated so it applies to the reader]
   → [What you'd change if it were yours]
```

The honest limitation: those nine posts pulled 136,448 impressions and produced 106 new follows. Big reach, weak conversion into audience. A teardown is interesting once. It doesn't give anyone a reason to stick around.

## 3. Giving away something you built internally

31 posts handing over a real internal asset: a prompt stack, a research process, a workflow, a system doc. Averaged 11,257 impressions, and this is where the audience came from.

Those 31 posts produced 768 of our 1,088 new follows, so 71%, and 5,353 of our 7,252 replies, so 74%. Roughly 6% of the posts generated three quarters of the growth.

**Examples:**

- [Jun 18](https://x.com/lorenzo_pravata/status/2067703025368449396) — 36,201 impressions, 479 saves, 557 replies, **116 follows**, 311 profile visits. Best single post of the quarter on every intent metric.
- [Jul 1](https://x.com/lorenzo_pravata/status/2072379243602780492) — 22,672 impressions, 346 saves, 474 replies, 102 follows
- [Jun 13](https://x.com/lorenzo_pravata/status/2065902544224702798) — 22,683 impressions, 358 replies, 71 follows
- [Jun 11](https://x.com/lorenzo_pravata/status/2065168352541077598) — 30,590 impressions, 261 replies

The reason it beats the teardown on follows is that a resource is a reason to stay in case there's another one. It also has a shelf life, which is the whole decay section further down. Note that all four of those examples are from June, and that's not me cherry-picking.

```
[Tool or method] is [strong claim] for [one specific job in your work]
[What you built, with a number attached: N prompts, N steps, N phases]
[One credibility anchor, stated once and never repeated]
Inside:
- [Component, phrased as an outcome rather than a feature]
- [Component]
- [Component]
[Access mechanic]
```

Nothing in that template mentions working together. Neither does the teardown. Formats one and five are where you sell, everything else earns the right to.

## 4. Long-form articles published on-platform

We published 34 of these in the quarter, where the platform renders a card and the post body is just the link. They did 264,450 impressions, 18.6% of the quarter's reach, averaging 7,777 against 3,470 for every other original post. So 2.2x.

The open rate is what matters. 3.48% expand rate against 1.18% for everything else, roughly triple, and on an article an expand is someone opening the piece. They also produced 278 shares, 30% of every share the account got, off 6% of the posts.

**Examples, sorted by opens:**

- [Jun 27](https://x.com/lorenzo_pravata/status/2070892271822578105) — 21,331 impressions, **1,437 opens** (6.7% open rate), 196 saves, 33 shares
- [Jul 25](https://x.com/lorenzo_pravata/status/2081050439252906419) — 22,681 impressions, **1,092 opens** (4.8%), 423 saves, 53 shares
- [Jul 8](https://x.com/lorenzo_pravata/status/2074842797840998543) — 13,137 impressions, 654 opens (5.0%), 294 saves
- [Jun 19](https://x.com/lorenzo_pravata/status/2068036848266489911) — 18,790 impressions, 521 opens

Now the other half of the picture. Across all 34 articles: 0.50% like rate, about half a normal post, 104 replies, and 17 new follows. Articles get opened, saved and forwarded. They don't get liked, argued with, or followed.

That's not a weakness. Someone opening 2,000 words and forwarding it to a colleague is doing something much closer to buying behaviour than someone liking a clip. What articles won't do is grow your account, so stop measuring them against posts that do.

```
[Title naming one specific mechanism, not a topic]
[Opening: the state of things right now, with a number in it]
[One section per component, in the order someone would execute them]
[One worked example carried all the way through]
[The failure modes, named]
[What to do first if they start Monday]
```

Pattern worth stealing: those three top-opening articles all ran between 4.8% and 6.7% expands and all three promised a single mechanism. The ones promising a list of things got impressions and didn't get opened.

## 5. Offering something in exchange for a comment

Six posts in the quarter offered a specific thing if you commented. Those six produced 686 replies, and off the back of them we sent 107 DMs. 107 conversations opened in twelve weeks, from six posts.

**Examples:**

- [Jul 18](https://x.com/lorenzo_pravata/status/2078611205313151366) — 18,245 impressions, 263 replies, 35 follows. Teardown and gated offer in one post, which is the highest-leverage combination in the whole set.
- [Jun 11](https://x.com/lorenzo_pravata/status/2065168352541077598) — 30,590 impressions, 261 replies
- [Jul 15](https://x.com/lorenzo_pravata/status/2077528202696618052) — 4,809 impressions, 81 replies — same offer, second run
- [Aug 12](https://x.com/lorenzo_pravata/status/2087582340683866566) — 2,342 impressions, 34 replies — same offer, third run

Those last three are the same offer run three times across two months. Reach dropped each time and it kept producing comments, and nobody complained, because the people seeing your account on a given day are a different slice of the same audience. If one of these works, running it once is the mistake.

What made the format work, in order of importance. The thing offered already existed, because we were documenting work we were doing anyway rather than building assets for content. The gate was a comment rather than a link, so the interaction stays on-platform. And delivery was manual — [here's what that looks like](https://x.com/lorenzo_pravata/status/2066536080485003383) — because a DM you send by hand is a conversation and an automated link is a download.

```
[Specific thing you built or found, with what's inside it listed]
[Why it exists: the real reason, which is usually that you needed it]
[Comment gate]
```

## 6. The pitch with the client's name swapped out

Six posts took the teardown structure and framed it as what we'd do if that company hired us. Average 3,404 impressions, so lower reach than a straight teardown, and the highest intent of anything we posted.

The mechanism is that the reader mentally inserts their own company into the slot. You're publishing a pitch and it doesn't read as one, because it's addressed to somebody else.

**Examples:**

- [Jun 9](https://x.com/lorenzo_pravata/status/2064386799602995258) — 5,041 impressions, 19 profile visits
- [Jul 20](https://x.com/lorenzo_pravata/status/2079246318191403496) — 4,416 impressions, 81 saves (1.83% save rate, well above the 0.94% baseline), 25 profile visits
- [Jul 14](https://x.com/lorenzo_pravata/status/2077042922348835314) — 3,889 impressions, 53 saves

```
If [company] were our client, here's what I'd do right now:
   → [What they sell and roughly what scale, from public information]
   → [What's already working, credited honestly]
   → [The gap, named specifically]
   → [The three things you'd do, in order]
```

Credit what's working before you name the gap. A teardown that's only criticism reads as someone auditioning, and it kills the authority the format was supposed to buy you.

## 7. Showing the work with the reasons attached

23 posts putting up something we'd actually made, with a short explanation of why it works. Averaged 5,609 impressions, 0.83% save rate. Another 29 posts explained a mechanism without the artifact, averaging 6,520 with a 1.10% save rate.

**Work plus reasons:**

- [Jun 4](https://x.com/lorenzo_pravata/status/2062524993615937566) — 19,985 impressions, 172 saves, 54 profile visits
- [Jul 7](https://x.com/lorenzo_pravata/status/2074503387605795290) — 30,511 impressions, 103 saves, 46 profile visits
- [Aug 20](https://x.com/lorenzo_pravata/status/2090384597737508973) — 4,380 impressions, 119 saves. **2.72% save rate, the best of any post in the quarter.** Three numbered reasons, one of them a craft detail nobody else would have noticed.

**Mechanism without the artifact:**

- [Jun 29](https://x.com/lorenzo_pravata/status/2071627754269745171) — 18,087 impressions, 281 saves
- [Jul 27](https://x.com/lorenzo_pravata/status/2081714788678148476) — 10,131 impressions, 174 saves (1.72% save rate)

Interesting that the mechanism-only posts got saved more on average than the ones with the work attached. My read: the artifact gets watched, the mechanism gets implemented, and people save what they intend to use. Which argues for never posting the work without the structure underneath it — and the Aug 20 post is the proof, because it's the one that did both.

```
[The thing you made]
The [N] reasons it works:
1. [Structural reason]
2. [Reason about the format or execution]
3. [Reason about the specific craft detail most people would miss]
```

The third item is where the credibility lives. Anyone can say the script was good. Naming the detail nobody else would notice is what proves you built it.

## 8. One-line replies on other people's posts

154 posts in the quarter were short replies on other accounts. Combined reach: 3,329 impressions. Average of 21 impressions each.

By every dashboard metric that's nothing, and it has the best profile-visit rate of any format we ran at 7.81 per thousand impressions against a 4.00 baseline. Tiny volume, best rate.

I'm not linking individual ones because there's nothing to see in any single reply, and that's the point. They average six or seven words. The value is entirely in doing it 154 times.

It isn't a reach play. It's how you stay resident with the thirty or forty people in your niche who send referrals. Operators talk to each other constantly, and when one of them gets a request they can't service, the name that surfaces is the one they saw yesterday. You don't build that with a newsletter. You build it with a one-line reply every day for a year, and it costs twenty minutes.

## The decay, and what actually caused it

Reach per post fell about 40% across the quarter while output went up. The easy conclusion is that the algorithm turned on us. The data says something more specific.

Originals only, by month:

| | June | July | August |
|---|---|---|---|
| Posts | 128 | 131 | 108 |
| Mean impressions | 4,841 | 3,684 | 2,940 |
| **Median impressions** | **1,633** | **2,016** | **1,719** |
| 90th percentile | 16,058 | 7,962 | 7,961 |
| Best post | 45,080 | 30,511 | 15,702 |
| Posts over 10k | 19 | 10 | 7 |

The median didn't move. June 1,633, August 1,719, July higher than both. The typical post never got worse. What collapsed was the top: the 90th percentile halved, the best post of the month fell 65%, and posts clearing 10k went from nineteen to seven.

That rules out the account being throttled. If distribution were suppressed the median falls with everything else. The floor held and the ceiling caved in.

So where did the ceiling go. Format three was carrying it:

| Giving away internal assets | June | July | August |
|---|---|---|---|
| Posts | 9 | 12 | 6 |
| Average impressions | 21,661 | 9,051 | 3,895 |
| Replies | 2,383 | 2,193 | 383 |
| New follows | 367 | 324 | 34 |
| Share of that month's follows | 75% | 64% | 36% |

82% drop in average reach, same format, same mechanic, ten weeks. Watch it happen post by post: [Jun 18](https://x.com/lorenzo_pravata/status/2067703025368449396) did 36,201 impressions and 116 follows. [Jul 1](https://x.com/lorenzo_pravata/status/2072379243602780492) did 22,672 and 102. By August the same format was averaging under 4,000.

In June it was supplying three quarters of our audience growth. By August, a third, and not because we posted it less.

That's the decay. One format saturated, and because it was carrying most of the outcome, every account-level average moved with it.

Why it saturated is the lesson. Everyone in the niche started shipping the identical post: same shape of claim, same numbered resource, same comment gate. At three weeks old the format was information. By week twelve people had seen forty versions and the one they'd already saved was still sitting in their bookmarks. Demand for the thing never dropped. The marginal version stopped being new.

**What to do with that.** Rotate on a clock rather than on evidence of decline, because by the time the reach drop shows up you're ten weeks late. Six weeks is what our data suggests. Keep the promise identical and swap the deliverable. And stop reading account averages as health when one format is generating 75% of your outcome, because at that concentration the average is just that format's chart with extra steps.

Worth noticing which formats didn't decay: replying to your own posts, and replying to other people's. Neither depends on novelty. A reply under your own post doesn't get less effective because everyone's seen it done. That's a real argument for weighting the boring formats heavier than most people do.

## On recent platform changes

Being straight, because it affects how you use the above. I can't verify what X changed in ranking over the last few months. My reliable knowledge stops at the end of May 2026 and I don't have search in this session, so any changelog for June through August would be invented, and that's not something to put your name on.

What the first-party data supports without one: the median holding while the tail collapsed is evidence for format saturation and against account suppression. That argument is measured on your own account, which makes it stronger than anything inferred from someone's thread about an update.

One hypothesis worth testing rather than asserting. If the platform now deduplicates semantically similar posts the way ad platforms collapse near-identical creative into a single entity, then a format saturating across a whole niche would decay structurally and not just from audience fatigue, and the account-level pattern would look exactly like this. Testable on your own export. Say it as a hypothesis, not a finding.

## What to actually measure

Save rate, not likes. Clicks coming from your self-replies. DM conversations opened per week. Article opens.

Reach tells you less than you'd think, and likes tell you nothing about pipeline. The format producing 90% of our clicks has the worst engagement numbers in the account, which is the whole reason people miss it.

---

## Close

1.42 million impressions in a quarter. The teardowns brought the reach, the giveaways brought the audience, the articles let buyers go deep, six gated posts opened 107 conversations, and 90% of the clicks came from the format with the worst engagement in the account.

If your content isn't producing calls, my guess is you're running formats two and three well and skipping one and five entirely. Those are where the pipeline is, and they're both boring, which is exactly why the field is open.

## Short-form cuts

1. 90% of our link clicks came from 11% of our reach, from the format with the worst engagement numbers in the account.
2. Announcing that our work had been updated out-clicked asking for the call, every time. Best one did 67 clicks on 867 impressions.
3. Six posts produced 107 DM conversations in twelve weeks.
4. Long-form articles pulled triple the open rate and half the likes of a normal post. One did 1,437 opens.
5. Articles promising one mechanism get opened. Articles promising a list get impressions.
6. Our reach per post fell 40% and the median post never got worse. The ceiling collapsed, the floor held.
7. One format's average reach dropped 82% in ten weeks while it was producing three quarters of our follows. That's the whole decay story.
8. The two formats that never decayed are the two that don't depend on novelty.
9. Posts explaining a mechanism got saved more than posts showing the work. People save what they intend to use.
10. Same gated offer, run three times across two months, produced comments every time.
