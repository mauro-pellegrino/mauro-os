## The one claim

The input is already sitting in your recordings. The system is six skills in a fixed order plus a
seventh whose only job is keeping the other six honest as evidence lands.

---

## The information

### 1. The seven skills, in order

| # | Owns | Done when |
|---|---|---|
| 1 | what the article is about | 3 to 4 subjects presented, one picked |
| 2 | cover, title and first three lines as one unit | all three drafted together, scored 4/5 or better |
| 3 | the title itself | 5 candidates generated, scored, presented |
| 4 | the cover image and its generation prompt | archetype chosen, prompt written |
| 5 | the article body | full draft, every claim sourced to the input |
| 6 | the companion post and the measurement hook | companion copy written, keyword assigned |
| 7 | keeping the other six honest | corpus regenerated, skills reconciled |

**Order matters.** Subject before title. Title and cover together, never one and then the other.
Body last. Skills 3 and 4 are components of skill 2: draft them inside the first-screen pass, then
sharpen each. A title written alone and a cover chosen alone produce a first screen that says the
same thing twice.

### 2. What the input can be

A call transcript, a Miro board, a client debrief, a YouTube script, a Slack thread, an ad account
export, a pile of raw notes. The skills do not assume the input is organised. Skill 1 does the
extraction. This is the part the audience underestimates: they think they need to write something,
and they need to select something.

### 3. Declare the intent before anything else

Reach rules and convert rules are both real and they are different rules. Set by Mauro, 2026-09-01:

> "I would rather optimize some articles to go viral and others to convert than to just have
> singularity."

An article declares its intent first and is then judged on the matching metric. A reach article that
books nothing is not a failure. A convert article at 5,000 impressions is not a failure.

### 4. What converts, from our own account

Every converter documents a process we operate. Both failures teach a tool or narrate somebody
else's win. Lift ranges from 2.27x at the top to 0.30x at the bottom, `[observed, n=12]`.

And length does not transfer between lanes: the twelve converters all run 738 to 1,012 words, which
is the second-weakest band for reach.

### 5. The measurement hook

Skill 6 assigns a unique DM keyword. That keyword is the entire attribution path, because the UTM
Source field is filled on 4 of 541 bookings. `[measured: _corpus.md]`

### 6. The standing constraint

Every claim in the finished article traces back to the input document. Every number in the skills
traces to the corpus file, which traces to a script and a capture. Where the input has a gap and the
structure wants something in that slot, the skill writes `[NEEDS: x]` and leaves it visible. A gap
beats a plausible fabrication, in article bodies, briefs and reports equally.

### 7. What happens when a rule dies

The seventh skill regenerates the corpus and reconciles the other six as new evidence lands. A
`[measured]` rule that loses two within-account contradictions is downgraded to `[observed]`. Three
and it is deleted. The legacy version of the article skill currently carries two specs that are
contradicted by the measured data, and its header says so rather than quietly keeping both.
