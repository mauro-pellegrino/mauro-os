## The one claim

A swipe file is a dataset. The moment you treat it as a dataset you find out that most of what you
were about to conclude from it is not supported.

---

## The information

### 1. What actually gets captured

The corpus holds **41 X article captures, 38 of them carrying an impression figure**, captured
2026-09-01. Each capture is the verbatim body, a description of the thumbnail, and the metrics.
Two are quote tweets. One is a deliberate bad example. `[measured: _corpus.md dataset line]`

The capture rule is capture only, no judging. The judgement happens later, in bulk, against the
whole set. Judging at capture time is how a swipe file turns into a folder of things you already
agreed with.

### 2. The finding that changes how you read your own swipe file

**It is a winners-only sample.** Nobody browsed a feed and saved a random article. Every capture
was chosen because it performed. Three consequences, all of them uncomfortable:

- **There is no bottom to the corpus.** The lowest-impression capture is the tail of a winners set,
  not a failure.
- **Every median is a median of outliers.** "Engraving covers median 336,400" means that among
  articles that already worked, the engraving ones sat there. It does not mean an engraving cover
  produces 336,400.
- **Comparing the top of the set to the bottom compares great to good.** That comparison was made
  on 2026-09-01 and a title conclusion was drawn from it. The conclusion was invalid and it is
  recorded in the file as invalid. `[observed: _corpus.md selection-bias section]`

The only captures that can honestly be called flops are the four from the account being run as a
run rather than selected for performance, plus the one sent deliberately as a bad example.

So: **the corpus is good for what winners have in common. It cannot tell you what makes something
fail, because failures were never collected.**

### 3. What you do with it once you accept that

Two scripts read the corpus and regenerate the numbers: one extracts title features, one classifies
cover archetypes. Both get re-run after captures are added, and every rule in the writing skills
points back at the regenerated file rather than at somebody's memory of it.

The rule that follows: swipe **structure**, never lines. A line borrowed from a winner reads as
borrowed. A structure borrowed from thirty winners reads as competence.

### 4. The tag that keeps it honest

Every rule pulled from this corpus is a **reach** rule, because all 41 captures carry impressions
and none carry bookings. That makes it the right evidence base for something written to travel and
the wrong one for something written to book a call. Tagging each rule at the point of use is what
stops the two lanes contaminating each other.
