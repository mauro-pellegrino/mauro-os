# Skill: LinkedIn Long-Form Post

**Version:** 1.0
**Created:** 2026-10-07
**Input:** A voice note, a transcript, a real piece of work Mauro ran this week, an X post that performed, or an article already written
**Output:** One LinkedIn post, pure copy, 150 to 300 words, tagged with the vehicle it was built on, plus 3 hook options

**Scope.** This is the **text-only** LinkedIn post: no video, no dashboard screenshot, no designed asset. It is the format Mauro can ship from a voice note on any given day, and it is the one LinkedIn rewards. If the post needs media, this is the wrong file, go to `skills/content/long-form/_master.md` plus the matching subtype. If it needs a DM keyword and a downloadable asset, it is a lead magnet, go to `skills/lead-gen/lead-magnet/_master.md`.

---

## Why Long-Form on LinkedIn

Mauro's booked calls come through LinkedIn more than anywhere else, and the format that does the work there is the long text post. The feed gives a post room to carry a mechanism, and his ICP (established agency owners) is on LinkedIn during the working day with the budget to hire. A 15-word hot take does not prove he runs a system. A 250-word walk through the system does.

The constraint that shapes everything: **LinkedIn truncates the post after roughly the first 200 characters, about three lines on mobile, behind "see more".** Everything before that cut is the whole ad for the rest. [CALIBRATE: confirm the exact cut with Mauro, it moves with LinkedIn's layout.]

## Step 1: Intake

The content decides the format, never the other way around. Read the source all the way through before picking anything.

**Sources, in order of preference:**

1. **A voice note or transcript.** The default. His own words are the source of personality, and the fix for a flat line is always his raw phrasing, never invented colour.
2. **A real thing he ran this week.** A build, a weekly analysis, a client problem, a skill he wrote. Real work is the content engine.
3. **An X post that performed.** Rewrite it up a register, do not paste it. See `skills/content/article-origination.md` for how a performing post gets briefed into something longer.
4. **An article he already published.** One source, many audiences. Pull one section, not a summary of the whole thing.

**Save the source first.** Voice notes and transcripts go to `research/transcripts/maurojpelle/` with the date, per the fetch-once-save-always rule, before anything gets produced from them.

One voice note can carry two or three posts if it holds two or three distinct ideas. Do not force it. One is fine.

## Step 2: Pick the Vehicle

Pick from the runnable set in `skills/content/vehicle-library.md`. The three built for this length:

| Vehicle | Funnel | Use when |
|---|---|---|
| **Authority Long-Form** | MOF | He is teaching a mechanism he actually runs, with real numbers |
| **Growth Long-Form** | TOF/MOF | One scaling insight with a contrarian pivot |
| **Personal Long-Form** | TOF/MOF | A personal story tied to the actual work, never freeform opinion |

The other eight runnable vehicles (Story-Based Lessons, Structured Business Take, Step-by-Step Breakdown, Written Listicles, Short Valuable Take, Hard Opinions RAW, Experience on Tough Times, Series Based Posts) all work as text-only LinkedIn posts. Reach for them when the idea does not fit the three above.

**Cadence rule: do not reuse a vehicle inside one week.** With eleven in rotation that is more than two months before anything repeats.

**Tag the vehicle at the top of the handover.** That tag is how we learn which formats convert once there are 20 to 30 posts to read.

## Step 3: The Hook (the first 200 characters)

The only part most of the feed ever reads. Write three options, every time, and let Mauro pick.

**Hook rules**
- **Input-driven.** Lead with the strongest thing the piece actually has, not with a formula.
- **One idea.** A hook that needs a second clause to make sense is not a hook.
- **A real number in the first two lines when one exists.** Specificity is credibility. Bracket what is not confirmed, never invent it.
- **The mechanism stays below the fold.** The hook can state the finding flat ("There's no 10 second rule in the x algorithm", "A post with 100,000 views isn't an outlier"), that is his source-check shape and it works. What it cannot do is hand over the how, the why, or the steps. Added 2026-10-07 after the first batch: an earlier version of this rule said nothing may be resolved above the fold, which fought his actual openers.
- **No "most agencies", no "it's not X it's Y", no three-word staccato stack.** These three kill more LinkedIn hooks than anything else and they are already banned in `brand/voice.md`.

**Hook shapes that fit his voice** (all from `brand/voice.md`, do not invent new ones):
- **Operational stat opener.** A production or volume number that sets scale. "Every Monday I match every post we shipped against every call that got booked."
- **False expectation.** State what the reader assumes, then flip it. "You'd think the hard part is writing the posts."
- **Experience anchor.** "After a year of running this engine daily for a B2B agency,"
- **Honest callout.** Name the hard part straight. "This one is easy to understand and miserable to actually do."
- **Named example.** Credit a real brand, creator or tool by name.

## Step 4: The Body

**Shape:**

```
[Hook, 1 to 2 lines, above the fold]

[Setup, 2 to 3 sentences. What is happening, why it matters to an agency owner, and the operator credit that makes it his.]

[The mechanism. This is the post. Either a bullet block (one step per line) or three short paragraph blocks, one idea each.]

[One line that lands the point and stops.]
```

**Formatting**
- One idea per line, blank line between most lines. No wall of text, LinkedIn punishes it visually.
- Paragraphs of 1 to 3 lines. Never 5.
- Bullets are line-broken fragments, lowercase, no trailing periods, no explanation clause hanging off them. `long-form/_master.md` uses checkmark bullets on LinkedIn, keep it to one block per post and never two blocks in a row.
- No hashtags. No emojis. [CALIBRATE: confirm with Mauro whether a link goes in the post or in the first comment.]
- **Lowercase product names**, same as his raw posts: claude, x, linkedin, dm, yt, miro, notion. The LinkedIn register is full sentences and proper capitalization, and product names are the exception to it.
- 150 to 300 words. Under 150 it is a short-form post, use `skills/content/short-form/`. Over 300 it wants to be an article, use `skills/content/x-articles-POINTER.md`.

**Content rules**
- **Show the work, do not claim it.** The post should contain a thing he actually ran, with enough detail that an operator could copy it.
- **Mechanism over conclusion.** "Here's how it works:" then the steps. Handing over the real process is the whole trust play.
- **Specific over tidy.** Trade a clean generic line for a lived one only he could write. The non-native-speaker angle, the Monday analysis, the tagging, the messy middle.
- **Friction.** Something in the post should give a reader room to disagree. AI avoids disagreement, people do not.
- **Check the pairing, not just the lines.** `brand/claims.md` carries rules about which claims may not sit in the same piece. The live one: a post carrying the five-minute estimate cannot also close on "a time I made up is worse than no time at all". Two lines that each pass on their own can fail together, which is what splits one merged post into two.
- **Every number traced to `brand/claims.md`,** or bracketed as `[NEEDS: x]` for Mauro to fill. The agency's revenue, the $28k deal and the booked-call totals need his sign-off before they appear in public copy, and the agency's anchors are never his personal proof.
- **Never name the client or the agency.**
- **Never name a tool he does not run.** Claude Code, Miro, Notion, LeadShark and Calendly are documented. Python is not.

---

## Step 5: The Close

**End on the last real beat.** The point lands and the post stops. Ending on the final bullet is correct and normal.

Banned closers, all of them:
- A summary line that restates the post.
- A lesson generalised out of his own experience.
- "What about you?", "Curious what others think", or any engagement bait question.
- "That's the difference between X and Y."
- A reframe that turns a downside into the intended mechanism.

**CTA.** Default is none. The post earns the profile click and the profile does the selling. A soft CTA is allowed in exactly one case: the asset it points at already exists and is ready to send. A DM keyword without a built deliverable is a promise he cannot keep, and that rule is standing (`CLAUDE.md` section 7).

"Talk soon, Mauro." is an article sign-off. It does not go on a feed post.

## Step 6: The Gate

Nothing reaches Mauro unchecked. Every draft goes to the `gate` agent in `.claude/agents/` before he sees it: voice, claims, slop and format passes. Failures get redrafted and re-gated. Only passing work is shown.

Before handing to the gate, run the LinkedIn-specific self-check:

- [ ] Does the first 200 characters work alone, with nothing resolved?
- [ ] Em dashes: zero.
- [ ] Any "it's not X, it's Y" or any theatrical reframe: zero.
- [ ] Opens with "most agencies" or "most people": rewrite.
- [ ] Colons: 2 or 3 maximum in the whole post.
- [ ] Clipped short-phrase or subject-dropped fragment stacks, especially in the opener: fold into flowing sentences.
- [ ] Any short line that just restates or labels a point ("That's the system."): fold it into the sentence around it with commas.
- [ ] Last line: if it summarises, teaches, or asks the reader anything, delete it and end on the beat before.
- [ ] Every number traced to `brand/claims.md` or bracketed.
- [ ] Every distinctive phrase traceable to something Mauro actually said in the source.
- [ ] Read it out loud. Would he say this to another operator over coffee?

## Anti-Patterns

- **Resolving the idea above the fold.** The hook gives away the payoff and nobody expands.
- **A wall of text.** It reads as effort on a desktop and as a brick on a phone.
- **Teaching a system he has not run.** Theory is what the gurus sell and his whole edge is being in it daily.
- **Borrowing the agency's numbers as his own proof.** A claims failure even when the number is real.
- **Reposting an X post unchanged.** Different room, different register. Rewrite it.
- **An engagement-bait question closer.** The fastest way to look like every other LinkedIn account.
- **A DM keyword with nothing built behind it.**
- **Checkmark bullets stacked three blocks deep.** One block, then prose.
- **Hashtags.** They do nothing here and they date the account.
