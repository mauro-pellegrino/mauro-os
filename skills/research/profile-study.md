# Profile study: the four-step process for analysing an account

**Source:** Mauro's loom to Juan, 2026-09-15. This file is that recording written down. His
wording is kept where it was deliberate.

**What it is for.** Pick an account that is clearly working, take it apart, and come out with
vehicles we can run on our own accounts and hand to clients. It is the same shape as the article
process we already run, pointed at a whole profile instead of one piece.

**Who it serves.** Three at once. Us, our clients, and the community. Mauro on the call: this
becomes very good when we have several clients, and people will pay for what comes out of it.

**It is also content.** See "Borrowed authority" at the bottom. The study is the asset, not just
the input to one.

---

## The four steps

Written on the board in this order. Step 3 is the one that decides whether the study is worth
anything.

```
1  STUDY CONTENT STRATEGY        copy and extract everything
2  UNDERSTAND SUCCESS + OUTLIERS why it works, and the 80/20 of it
3  BREAK DOWN                    the deep read, not the obvious one
4  TEMPLATIZE VEHICLES           for ourselves and for clients
```

**On the word "steal".** It was written as "steal" first and corrected on the call. It is
**study**. The distinction is not politeness. Stealing copies the output. Studying takes the flow,
which is the thing that transfers.

**On "templatize".** His article type is a template. He follows a flow, and that flow is what we
can run ourselves and show a client. We are after the repeatable shape, not the post.

---

## Step 1 · Study the content strategy

Copy and extract all the content and all the valuable information.

The prompt and the field list live in `profile-extraction.md`. Use it rather than copy-pasting by
hand.

**Window.** The last month is enough. More if the account allows it. These profiles do not post
that much daily, so a month is a real sample rather than a snapshot.

**Four things that will slow you down, all hit on the first run:**

- **X truncates at "Show more".** You can click through them, and it is slow. Decide up front
  whether you need full bodies or opening lines.
- **Character limits on paste.** A month of a prolific account will not go into one chat window.
  It gets cut silently. Extract to a document first, then read the document in pieces.
- **Scrolling far back is unreliable.** Going back to June and holding it is a fight. Take what
  loads cleanly and say where you stopped.
- **Articles are the only thing with visible view counts.** You used to be able to see which tweet
  was the biggest. You cannot now. Articles still show views, so they are where outliers are
  findable without an export.

**Practical:** run it with the profile open in the tab. The Chrome extension reads the window you
are on, which is why it knows whose profile it is.

## Step 2 · Understand success and outliers

Why it works for him, and which part of what he does is carrying it. The 80/20.

An outlier is a piece that is obviously far ahead of the rest. Find them first, then ask what they
have in common that the median pieces do not.

This step is arithmetic. It is the last easy one.

## Step 3 · Break it down

**The most important step.** Getting the content is something anyone can do. This is where the
study becomes worth something.

**You decide what the model looks at.** This is a judgement call and it will be different for every
account. The questions below are the ones from the call. Add to them per account.

```
why so many impressions
how many reposts per article
which topics
which images
videos, actually
which offer is he pushing
what is he selling
how many formats, how many posts per day
```

**And the one that matters most:**

```
what makes his account pop as much as it does
can we reverse-engineer it
what can we learn from his performance, in terms of content
what can we templatize for other industries, for ourselves, and for clients
```

### Superficial versus deep, and why the difference decides everything

**Superficial** is "his article looks like this and it works like this". It is fine and it is not
worth much. It is the chill version.

**Deep** is asking why the account works at all.

Mauro's own examples from the call:

- Why does everything on this account get so many impressions?
- Why does the same thing happen on Ronin's account?
- Why does a plain text post with no media get 13,000 views?
- Ronin has about 500 YouTube subscribers and no videos on the channel, and it still works. So
  what is he actually doing?

**What he used to do, and what stopped being enough:** looking at what got posted, how much it
got, and what it was about. That is three descriptive facts. It tells you nothing about the
mechanism.

## Step 4 · Break down and templatize the vehicles

**Goal, as written on the board:** synthesise all learnings, superficial and deeper, into
applicable pieces, information and vehicles for our own accounts and for general strategy.

A vehicle is a repeatable shape with a trigger, a structure and a format. "This image is great" is
not a vehicle. "When someone ships a tool our ICP uses, quote it inside a day with a numbered
how-to and a carousel" is.

**Every vehicle gets a verdict**, because not all of them transfer:

- **adopt** — runs as-is on our accounts
- **adapt** — the mechanism transfers, something about it has to change
- **reject** — it works for them and will not work for us, with the reason written down

A rejected vehicle with a reason is worth more than three adopted ones with none. It is the part
that proves the study was a study.

Vehicles are written in the format of `skills/content/vehicle-library.md` so an approved row
pastes straight across. Nothing enters that file without Mauro's sign-off, row by row.

## Borrowed authority: why the study is itself content

When someone posts about Alex Hormozi or Dan Koe, they are borrowing that person's authority. The
audience already knows the name, so the name does the work of getting attention.

**Two forms, and both work:**

**The name.** "I stole Dan Koe's strategy, broken down." The name is the hook.

**The number.** "I broke down 40 articles." Quantity does the same job, and a numeral pulls the eye
on its own.

You can use both in one line.

This is why the account chosen matters. It has to be an account the audience already places. If
they do not recognise it, there is no authority to borrow and the study has to stand on its
findings alone.

**The number form is stronger and it compounds**, because running this process several times is
what produces the number.

## What is actually hard

Not the extraction. That is slow and it is solvable.

**Understanding what comes back, well enough to apply it.** Juan raised this on the call and Mauro
agreed it is the hard part. A model will return an analysis. Reading it, disagreeing with parts of
it, and turning it into something you would actually run is the work.

So the output of step 3 gets read by a person and argued with. It does not get pasted into step 4.

## Output convention

```
research/profile-studies/<handle>-<YYYY-MM-DD>-raw.md        step 1, never edited after it lands
research/profile-studies/<handle>-<YYYY-MM-DD>-analysis.md   steps 2 and 3
research/profile-studies/vehicles-from-<handle>.md           step 4, with verdicts
research/profile-studies/<handle>-<YYYY-MM-DD>-stats.py      any number that got computed
```

Every script states its own limit in its first lines. Every number in the analysis traces to the
script or to the raw file.
